About

I build systems, teach people, and develop leaders.

That has been the work for nearly twenty years, in classrooms, startups, and executive teams. The job titles changed along the way. The thread never did.

The story

From the classroom to the executive team, and back to teaching.

TeachBuildLeadAdvise

I didn't start in software. I started in a classroom.

My undergraduate degree is in education, and I spent time working with students in public school classrooms before software became my career. That might sound like a detour. It turned out to be the foundation. Teaching forces you to understand how people actually learn, how to break a complicated idea into pieces someone can use, and how to tell the difference between a person who is stuck and a person who is about to break through. I have used those skills every day since, just with different students.

I had been writing code since the late nineties, and after college I started my first company building websites and web applications for local businesses. One company turned into several. I co-founded a web consultancy, an enterprise software company, and, more recently, a marketplace for small businesses and a small game studio. Running your own company teaches you things no job can: how to make decisions with incomplete information, how to sell, and how much of "technical" success is really about people and priorities.

I also spent years inside growing software companies, including AdvancedMD, Jane, and Route. At Route I managed multiple teams of more than fifty engineers and QA while the company grew from a startup into a $1B company in three years. At AdvancedMD, as VP of Engineering and Partnerships, I owned the partnerships and integrations department, leading engineering, product, and support for the company's API and partner integrations and working with the executive team on build, partner, or buy decisions. As SVP of Technology at Clicklease, I lead the full technology organization: engineering, IT, DevOps, information security, and the service desk.

Somewhere in there I picked up an MBA, because I kept finding that the hardest engineering decisions were actually business decisions wearing a technical costume.

Today I work as an advisor, fractional technology leader, and educator. I help companies use AI where it actually creates value, strengthen engineering organizations that have outgrown their habits, and develop the technical leaders who make everything else possible. I still build, too. I co-founded Gatherly Lane, a marketplace that connects small businesses with customers who will love them, and D4rk Rebel Games, where I get to mix my engineering background with a favorite pastime.

The common thread has never been a technology or a title. It's been building systems, teaching people, developing leaders, and helping organizations solve problems that matter.

How I work

A few things I believe about teams, learned the hard way.

  • Trust and respect come first.

    When trust and respect are central to a team's culture, people build each other up, skills deepen, and business goals can be aligned with individual interests. That culture is built deliberately or not at all.

  • Clear expectations are a kindness.

    People deserve to know what success looks like and how they're doing against it. Expectations should be written down, measurable, agreed on, and revisited. Nobody should be left wondering.

  • Candor and empathy aren't opposites.

    The most useful feedback is direct and kind at the same time. And when someone on the team goes quiet, that silence is usually a signal that something doesn't feel safe to say.

  • Stretch people, then scaffold them.

    People grow most when they're challenged just past their current ability and supported while they're there. That's true for engineers, managers, and executives alike. I wrote about this.
